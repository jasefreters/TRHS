# Reset Program: guidance for changes

Static site on GitHub Pages (`main`, root) with a Supabase database. Plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step. `index.html` is the student laptop view, `teacher.html` the dashboard. Settings in `js/config.js`, student-facing words in `js/content.js`, electives in `js/modules/`, database in `supabase/schema.sql`. See `README.md` for how the program runs.

## Decisions made by the staff

- **No personal information in the database or the repo.** Students are random 8-character codes; staff keep the code-to-name list offline. Never add name or email fields. Module written answers are stored at staff request (see below); ask before storing any other free text. The certificate name is typed on the page and never saved.
- **Students don't log in.** Their code is the key, used only through the `student_*` database functions. Teachers log in with Supabase Auth and must be listed in `public.staff`.
- **Modules, everywhere:** everything in the day is a module. **Group modules** (a staff member with the group) and **individual modules**, which are either **independent** sessions (on the laptop) or **teacher-led** sessions (one-on-one with a staff member). Use these words on both sites (`RP.KIND_LABEL`). The plan editor is called the **Daily schedule**.
- **Day shape:** 10 sessions, each ending with a school bell or mid-period (P1 8:20–9:23, P2 –10:26, B1 10:26–10:56, P3 –11:59, P4 –1:02, B2 1:02–1:32, P5 –2:35). Session 1 (8:20–8:50) is usually the whole-group morning session (staff can drag **Morning session** to a later time for a late arrival or a return meeting; 8:20 then becomes free for anything): social story, then the morning check-in (ready-to-learn traffic light, have I had breakfast / pen / uniform / bag / lunch / other food, then the survey); the board shows the traffic light and anything they don't have. Session 10 (2:03–2:35) is usually the whole-group afternoon debrief with the afternoon survey (it can be dragged to another time too, like the morning session). Staff can change a day's session times on the daily schedule (⏱ Times; saved per student and day in `visits.times`, e.g. for Tuesday's shorter bell times; `RP.TIMETABLE_PRESETS` holds named sets of bell times: **Tuesday** has an assembly / year meeting after P2 (a row with `event: true`, which students go to), B1 at 10:41, shorter periods and no B2 or P5, so the debrief moves to 12:45), optionally for everyone in the program that day. The same dialog adds sessions (＋ splits a session in two, e.g. three 20-minute sessions in a period instead of two) and removes breaks and unstarted sessions (✕), e.g. B2 and P5 on a short day (a removed afternoon debrief moves to the last session left); added sessions use slots 12–20 and are ordered by the day's times (`RP.bySchedule`), never by slot number. The run sheet shows them in the period they fall in, and staffing follows the normal timetable's rows. Sessions 2–9 are half-periods. The breaks are called **B1** and **B2** (not recess and lunch); students go as normal and come back to **the Hub** (the room's name).
- **New students start with empty independent sessions** (`RP.DEFAULT_ELECTIVES` is empty) for staff to fill in; group modules come from the rotation.
- **Two-day rotation:** the room alternates group-session rotations, so students on Day 1 and Day 2 share group sessions. Every core group session must be done within the student's two days, so **each group session and each independent module must stand alone**: there's no guaranteed order.
- **Group modules** (max 5 students, 1 staff member; run sheets in `RP.GROUP_SESSIONS[].guide`, slides in `.slides` for the Present view): Rotation A: T-RED Values, RIOT, Wellbeing, Chain Analysis. Rotation B: Student Code of Conduct, RIOT, Classroom Expectations, Self-Regulation. Both at 8:50, 9:55, 11:27 and 12:30. **Every guide follows the Thomas Reddall Lesson Routine** (`RP.LESSON_ROUTINE`; poster at `img/lesson-routine.jpg`): entry, Do Now, learning intention and success criteria, explicit teaching, brain break, application, recap, exit. Make them interactive (butcher's paper, discussion, card sorts). T-RED uses discussion and butcher's paper but no games (those were for orientation day). Classroom Expectations teaches the lesson routine itself. **RIOT** (`js/riot.js`) is one group module with eight lessons condensed to 25 minutes from the school's RIOT PowerPoints and student workbook (keep the RIOT wording). On the daily schedule staff choose the lesson and the worksheet format: paper (print from Session guides), a PDF on the laptop (`resources/riot/`, made by `tools/riot-pdfs.js` from `riot-worksheet.html`), or interactive on the laptop. Every worksheet exercise has a matching slide (placed after the teaching slide given by `at`), and the slides use a RIOT theme like the original PowerPoints. When a student does a lesson on the laptop, the slides sit beside the worksheet and follow the exercise they're on (`riotSplit` in `js/student.js`; `riot-slides.html` opens them in a new tab), so they can do it on their own if no staff member is free (staff can turn the slides off per session on the daily schedule: `state.slides === false`); staff can also drag a RIOT lesson into an independent session (it counts as the core RIOT module). The answers for every lesson are on Session guides (`RP.riotAnswersMarkup`; printable at `riot-worksheet.html?lesson=N&answers=1`). RIOT 1 and RIOT 2 are retired (kept for older plans). Wellbeing and Self-Regulation are still outlines. Use made-up examples; never ask a student to share their own incident. Staff can drag a different group module into a slot, but only after an "Are you sure?" confirm. **RAGE Recap** is an optional (not core, not in a rotation) group module staff swap in when it suits the group; it's an outline until the school's RAGE materials are added.
- **Other sessions:** staff can drag **Other** into a slot and type what it is (kind `free`, activity `custom`, the typed name in `title`). Students see the name and are told they won't need their laptop. The staff ticks it done.
- **Teacher-led sessions** are dragged into a slot by the teacher (kind `one_to_one`, `RP.INDIVIDUAL_SESSIONS`): Day 1 **Teacher-led: My Reset Plan** (was "Review of plans, goals and Sentral"); Day 2 **Teacher-led: My Return Plan**; **Return from suspension meeting**; classwork and assessment check-in; learning and support check. One student at a time: the daily schedule and board warn about clashes. The staff member is whoever is on the run sheet's staffing for that session (`public.staffing`). The two plans each have an **independent** version the student does first on the laptop (`reset-plan`: the Behaviour Chain Analysis worksheet from SocialWorkersToolbox.com, wording unchanged as its licence requires, printable copy in `resources/`; `module-6`: My Return Plan, which is the school's **Student Return to Class Plan** from the Head Teacher Wellbeing/Learning Support, in her wording, six sections); the teacher-led page shows those answers once submitted. Each has a page showing the student's relevant surveys and module answers; My Return Plan and the return meeting print the Student Return to Class Plan to sign, in the same six sections and filled in from the student's answers (staff can edit ticks and answers before printing; nothing typed there is saved). **Teacher-led modules** (`teacherLed: true`, e.g. `understanding-truancy` from the Learning Support Teacher, in her wording) are stored as independent sessions because the student works through them on their laptop, but they're labelled and planned as teacher-led. With `live: true` the student's answers so far are saved with their progress (`state.draft`, removed when they submit) so the staff member's page shows them as they type, with a staff-only "Talk about it" prompt (`talk`) per activity, a Sentral checklist (attendance, positive and negative entries) and a staff check-in to print (not saved). Session notes and testing results stay in Sentral and other school systems, not here.
- **Movement breaks** are not planned into the day. Each group module has a brain break built in, and written movement-break resources (the `move-*` entries) sit on the Session guides page for staff to pull out when students need a break.
- **Surveys** (`js/surveys.js`): start and end of both days (`d1am`, `d1pm`, `d2am`, `d2pm`) plus a follow-up 4–6 weeks after return (a "followup" program that reactivates the code for the survey only). Same 10 rating statements in each, from the school's TRHS survey. Surveys done at the same time must take the same time (both mornings match, both afternoons match). Wording asks students not to give identifying information. Ratings go in the session's `result` (`survey`, `ratings`, `total`), written answers in `state.responses`; the Reports page and downloads use them.
- **Sentral post:** the **Sentral post** button on a student's record writes a short post for the Sentral staff noticeboard (`sentralFacts` and `sentralText` in `js/teacher.js`): one line of context, then dot points led by the **common threads** (ideas that recur in their answers, `STRATEGIES`; what their modules kept coming back to, `THEMES`), then growth, highlights and a very low area to support, and one of their own responses (staff choose; obvious spelling slips fixed by `tidy`). Keep it short (about 60–90 words, at most four points) and not a list of everything they did. So posts don't read like a template, the wording and layout are picked by a seed from the student and **↻ New version** (same facts, different wording). Staff type the name and choose pronouns (they/he/she); neither is saved.
- **Pause:** a program can be paused (student absent); the code shows a "paused" screen until resumed.
- **Numeracy** is two half-hour modules, `numeracy-day1` and `numeracy-day2`. Each level is an NSW Stage: Level 1 = Stage 1 (Years 1–2) up to Level 5 = Stage 5 (Years 9–10). Levels 2 and 3 (Stages 2 and 3) come from The Daily Dash (`jasefreters/mathswarmup`): `tools/dash-numeracy.js` converts its picture-free questions into `js/modules/numeracy-dash.js` (12 per level per day, enough for 30 minutes; rerun it when the Dash grows). Levels 1, 4 and 5 are written in the module files. Students and staff see only "Level N" and a short topic: the site never shows the Stage or Years (staff know Level N is Stage N), not even in the staff preview or the Suggest level list. Credit the NSW Department of Education (CC BY 4.0).
- **Suspension Booklet** (`suspension-booklet`): students pick a subject, type the page or question and their answer, and mark subjects started or finished; the day certificate lists them. Each year's booklet is split into one PDF per subject in `booklets/yearN/` **without the teacher answer sheets**; students can also use a paper copy or their own booklet. Only Year 7 so far.
- **Reading levels 1–5** (Stages 1–5, labelled "Level" not "Stage") are **only in the Literacy module** (Numeracy's levels are Stages too, above); other modules use their standard text. Nothing reads aloud automatically, but every page has a Read aloud button. Students can look up any word with **What does it mean?** in the top bar (select a word, or type one; only the word is sent to a free dictionary).
- **Chromebooks:** students log in as themselves and use Chrome. They can't print, so certificates and answers offer Save as PDF and Email.
- **Videos** must suit students (Years 7–10, mostly 7–8), not adults. Staff should preview each one before students use it; every video item has a written summary in case it won't play.
- **Electives are done independently** on the laptop, so the teacher is free for 1:1s. Group sessions tell students to put the laptop aside; the teacher ticks them off.
- A finished elective can't be redone unless the teacher clicks **Let them redo**.
- **Records kept for returning students:** electives finished, level and score, group sessions done.
- **Staff can follow answers live.** At staff request, every module (not surveys) saves the answers so far with its progress (`state.draft`, sent a couple of seconds after the student stops typing, removed when they submit), so staff can open **Answers** on the daily schedule or **See answers** on the board. Questions with a right answer (multiple choice, sort, order, match, typed answers) show how many goes it took (`state.attempts`: goes until right; `state.revealed`: wrong goes before "Show me the answer"), during and after the module, RIOT lessons included. Once submitted, the morning check-in and afternoon debrief also have **Answers** (`renderSurveyAnswers`: ready-to-learn light and missing items, each rating with the change since their first survey, written answers). When the module is finished, the answers are saved (`sessions.state.responses`), locked, and shown on the student's finished page and the teacher's record page. Students are told their teacher will read them. Prompts stay optional, as the staff notes ask not to force disclosure.
- **Certificates are given at the end of each day, not each module.** The day certificate lists every module finished that day (group, independent, teacher-led). Students get it after the afternoon survey and can reopen it from their plan; teachers reprint it from the record. There's also one **completion certificate** for the whole program (`RP.programModules`, `cert.complete`): students get it after the Day 2 afternoon survey, and staff can tick it on Print certificates instead of, or as well as, the day ones. Finishing a module shows the student's answers to save or email. Names are typed at print time and never saved.
- **Independent modules** are as interactive as possible and vary activity types across the day (sort, order, match, select, rating, sliders, body map, choose-your-path stories, phrase tiles, breathing, chain-writing, video). `module-1`…`module-5` are the reflection halves of the school's module documents (Module 6 is now the teacher-led My return plan); `my-chain`, `tred-values` and `code-of-conduct-check` are built from the school's Chain Analysis video, T-RED presentations and the Department's Student Code of Conduct. My Reset Plan and My Return Plan (independent halves of the teacher-led plans), Suspension Booklet, Numeracy Day 1 and Day 2. Staff-chosen extras: Say It Better, Online Conflict and Social Media, Restorative Conversation Prep, Growth Mindset, Goal Builder, Healthy Habits Check, Asking for Help, Study Skills, Attendance and Truancy. **Literacy is one half-hour module** (`literacy.js`) with levels 1–5, built from the reading, vocabulary, spelling and grammar question banks in `literacy-*.js` (hidden as separate modules). Keep the school's wording when editing modules. Modules are kept short (about 20 minutes): pages marked `extra: true` are left out unless staff choose **Full version** on the daily schedule. In Literacy and Numeracy, a student who finishes a level is offered the next one (same session; `result.levels` keeps each level's score). **Friendships** covers healthy friendships, support networks and talking to friends. Staff can preview any independent module from the daily schedule, board and Session guides: **Try it as a student** runs the real module (nothing saved), and **Questions and answers** lists every page with the answers.
- **Branding:** colours from the school's colour hierarchy (maroon, light blue and dark navy primary; royal blue secondary; greys). The colour hierarchy image is a palette guide only: **never use it as a logo or picture on the site.** The logo is the sun rising over an open book (`RP.logo`, drawn as SVG). Motto: "Focusing on our Future" (footer, certificates, Present view). Navy top bar; dashed sunbursts (`img/sunburst.svg`) as soft background decoration.

## Before publishing

- Run the flows in a browser with `?demo=1` (teacher creates a student, plans the day, student completes an elective and gets the certificate). Check every answer in new elective content yourself.
- The run sheet prints on one landscape A4 page.
- Changes to the database go in a new SQL file under `supabase/` (e.g. `update-3.sql`) for staff to run in the SQL Editor; keep `schema.sql` as the full current setup. The site must keep working before an update is run (see `withV2` in `js/api.js`).
- Publishing: commit to `main` on `jasefreters/TRHS`. Bump the `?v=` number on every script and stylesheet tag in `index.html` and `teacher.html` so browsers load the new files straight away.
